# STEP 4 — Worked example: the two-brick question

**Brick A** at 20 °C in a 20 °C room. **Brick B** at 100 °C in the same room.
(a) Do both bricks emit electromagnetic radiation?
(b) Which emits more?
(c) For each brick, state the direction of **net** heat flow between it and the room air.
(d) Brick B slowly cools. What happens to that net flow?

**Answers given on the page:**

- (a) **Yes — both.** Every object emits electromagnetic radiation regardless of
  temperature (the universal statement from sharpened answer #8).
- (b) **B, the hotter brick.** Temperature sets the radiation's character: hotter
  objects emit at shorter wavelengths / higher frequencies and pour out more
  energy. At L01's level: "B emits more, and at different wavelengths."
- (c) **A:** same temperature as the room → already in thermal equilibrium →
  net flow = 0 (collisions and radiation continue both ways, but cancel).
  **B:** hotter than the room → net flow from B into the air — the fast side
  delivers more energy on average.
- (d) The temperature difference shrinks → the net flow shrinks → reaches zero
  when B hits room temperature. B's cooling curve matches the pre-class brick
  curve: fast at first, then slower — approach, not arrival.

# STEP 5 — Variations

**V1 · A 1000 kg iceberg (0 °C) and a 50 g nail (800 °C) touch. Which way does
heat flow — and does the iceberg's bigger total energy matter?**
→ Direction: nail → iceberg, set by the temperature difference alone. The
iceberg's far larger **total** energy is irrelevant to direction — "who has more
energy" never decides heat flow, "who is hotter" does. Eventually both share a
common temperature (closer to 0 °C than to 800 °C, because the iceberg is
enormously bigger — quantitative version comes later).
*Re-test tag: "direction ≠ total energy" trap — the student's review has not been
tested on it yet.*

**V2 · The review wrote: "energy transfer continues until the brick reaches
thermal equilibrium." A quiz asks: at the instant of equilibrium, state precisely
what is zero and what is not.**
→ Zero: the **net** energy flow (average molecular energies have matched).
Not zero: the individual two-way energy exchanges — collisions and radiation
continue in **both** directions forever. Equilibrium = balanced traffic, not an
empty road.

**V3 · The frost is gone by ~1 hr — where did the frost's water go, microscopically?**
→ Two-stage: while the surface was below 0 °C, vapour **deposited** onto it as
solid frost (the white layer). As the surface warmed past 0 °C, the frost
**melted** and then **evaporated** back into the air — phase changes driven by
the brick's rising temperature. Slides' "condensation" is the loose shorthand;
strict vocabulary: deposition → melt → evaporation.
